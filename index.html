How It Happens

Why Do Companies Keep Calling the Wrong Person?

Wrong-number robocalls can happen when a company has outdated or inaccurate contact information.

For example, you may obtain a new cell phone number that previously belonged to one of the company's customers, and the company may continue using that number in an attempt to reach the former subscriber. In other cases, a company may simply have the wrong number associated with one of its customers.

Even if the first call resulted from a mistake, you shouldn't have to endure repeated unwanted prerecorded calls intended for somebody else. The TCPA is a federal consumer-protection law that restricts companies from making certain artificial or prerecorded voice calls to cell phones without the required consent — and when companies violate those protections, affected consumers may have the right to pursue monetary damages.

Who Is Calling You?

Calls Meant for Someone Else

Wrong-number prerecorded calls can potentially come from many types of organizations, including:

Banks & Financial Institutions

Prerecorded calls about accounts or services — meant for their customer, not you.

Debt Collectors

Automated collection calls trying to reach the person who used to have your number.

Creditors

Prerecorded payment or account messages directed at someone else.

Insurance Companies

Recorded policy or claims calls sent to the wrong number.

Telemarketers

Prerecorded sales calls to a number you never gave them.

Loan Servicers

Automated loan messages intended for the previous subscriber.

Healthcare-Related Companies

Recorded appointment, billing, or account calls meant for another patient.

Retailers

Prerecorded promotions or account calls sent to the wrong person.

Other Businesses

Any company repeatedly calling your cell phone to reach somebody else.

Don't Delete Those Voicemails

If you're receiving unwanted calls, preserving information about them may help an attorney evaluate what happened. Consider saving:

  • Prerecorded voicemails, screenshots of incoming calls, and your call history
  • Dates and approximate times of the calls, and the phone numbers used to contact you
  • Text messages, if applicable
  • The name of the company calling, and notes about what the recorded message said
  • Records showing that you asked the caller to stop, if applicable
